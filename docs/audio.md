# Spatial audio

Open mod settings with **F8** on the default keyboard binding, choose **Audio**, then choose **Preview ahead**, **left**, **right**, **behind** or **above**. The settings dialog also accepts its configured controller navigation and confirm/back actions. **Mod sounds** and **HRTF** are independent and both default on. They persist in the NW profile. Turning sounds off prevents cues and previews and retains the HRTF preference. Returning to the root focuses **Audio** again. The speech master is in **Speech**. [Speech controls and restoration](implementation.md#speech-master) · [Entity soundscape](soundscape.md) · [Route beacon](beacon.md) · [Live cave cues](puzzles-and-secrets.md#live-crossing-cues) · [Feature register](feature-checkpoint.md)

The preview status reports the output mode selected for the cue. A request that loses its dialog or scene drops its status. The fallback pans left and right relative to the listener and lowers pitch to 72% of the forward pitch across the rear half-plane. HRTF uses the same original cue with a three-dimensional bearing and the built-in OpenAL Soft dataset. The listener's axes are right, up and forward. The bridge reverses the forward axis for OpenAL's default listener orientation. Compare front, behind and above with headphones during final gameplay testing. Native game audio is owned by the game.

## Ownership and cancellation

- [The renderer service](../src/runtime/audio.js) starts a single Node worker on demand after the owning dialog passes its readiness checks. One request may be in flight. Each of the six lanes holds at most one pending request. Each request sends a generation, three bearing components, a gain and the HRTF selection. Scene objects and readiness predicates stay on the renderer. Replacing a preview revokes its predecessor. A worker error closes admission and reports the output failure to the current dialog.
- [The plugin](../src/plugin.js) admits previews only for the focused, presented Audio dialog in the current scene. Group exit, sound/HRTF changes, settings retirement, native scene departure, resize, blur and page hide revoke admission before replacement work. The renderer only stores the closed generation. It never joins the worker or waits for the audio device.
- [The Windows addon](../src/services/windows/audio-addon.cpp) binds the narrow Node-API exports in the loaded NW `node.dll` and owns the SDK objects on the Node worker. It keeps a strong Buffer reference to seven shared 32-bit words for the six admission generations and wall deadline until the SDL stream has been destroyed. SDK entry points use application-relative absolute paths and constrained DLL lookup. The service declines a preloaded SDL3 or OpenAL Soft module of the same name. The DLLs remain separate from the addon. [Node 14 Node-API](https://nodejs.org/download/release/v14.12.0/docs/api/n-api.html#n_api_napi_get_buffer_info)
- [OpenAL Soft loopback](../src/services/windows/audio-hrtf.hpp) renders 48 kHz stereo into a bounded array on the worker, checking admission between 128-frame slices. It requires the runtime to report HRTF enabled and select its exact built-in dataset. The [cue bank](../src/services/windows/spatial-cue.hpp) contains original procedural sounds for previews, wall feedback, beacon, timed cave movement and seven entity cues. Both output modes use the same definitions.
- [The SDL stream](../src/services/windows/audio-device.hpp) is the one hardware output for this service. The callback checks the shared generation and mixes at most 1,024 source frames in subchunks of at most 256, checking again before each submission. SDL holds the recursive stream lock during the callback. The callback removes itself on completion or revocation so the idle stream has no recurring mod callback. The worker publishes completed PCM and owns stream destruction. [SDL callback locking](https://wiki.libsdl.org/SDL3/SDL_AudioStreamCallback) · [Callback removal](https://wiki.libsdl.org/SDL3/SDL_SetAudioStreamGetCallback) · [Recursive mutex semantics](https://wiki.libsdl.org/SDL3/SDL_LockMutex)

## Wall feedback and footsteps

In **F8 → Audio**, **Wall sounds** and **Footstep sounds** are independent and default off. Their starting volumes are 65 and 50 percent. **Shift+H** toggles wall sounds in the field. The Audio panel offers four directional wall previews and a footstep preview.

Wall cues are aligned to the movement keys. Up is north, right is east, left is west and down is south even when Wendy turns. Each blocked edge has its own pitch. From highest to lowest the order is north, east, west and south, following the Pixel Remaster accessibility mods. All blocked directions sound together. Each step refreshes their shared three-second hold. A disappearing obstacle retires its own voice while the others keep playing. Repeated attempts against a wall leave the timer running. Each successful step plays a dry heel and toe pair. The first pip has a lower, softer noise texture, and the toe starts 22 ms later. Each burst lasts up to 13 ms at the base playback rate. The pair alternates slightly left and right below Wendy. Both features use native walking, including ordinary autowalk. Vehicles and scripted movement remain under their native audio.

`Game_Player.moveByInput` supplies the movement outcome at original byte 222850 of `rmmz_objects.js`. Accepted event entry at 249454 revokes wall and footstep voices before native event lock. The wall owner checks up to four passage edges after movement and on a six-update cadence while its lease is live. It bounds active tile events at 32 and event slots at 513. Each blocked edge owns one generation. Continuing edges only extend the shared deadline. The timer checks stationary edges every six updates while it is active. The footstep observer publishes one short request for the current tile. Focus loss, native loading, menus, transfers and settings changes retire the affected output. [Wall owner](../src/world/wall-audio.js) · [Footsteps](../src/world/footsteps.js)

The audio worker spatializes four periodic 90 ms waveforms with approximately 289 Hz north, 244 Hz east, 200 Hz west and 167 Hz south. Stereo centers north and south, with the south tone lowered to about 122 Hz. Complete cycles keep the loops continuous. HRTF renders one warm-up period before the loop. The SDL callback mixes up to four wall loops, the footstep lane and the ordinary cue lane in one stream. Each lane has independent generation ownership. Each wall voice fades linearly to silence over 500 ms after three seconds of inactivity. A step during the fade restores the continuing loops smoothly. The SDL callback applies the envelope to the spatialized wall samples alone. Native phase boundaries still revoke playback immediately. The callback processes at most 1,024 frames in 256-frame chunks and unregisters when the last voice ends. A device may still play already-buffered samples. [Native cue bank](../src/services/windows/spatial-cue.hpp) · [SDL mixer](../src/services/windows/audio-device.hpp)

The native movement fixture verified all four obstacle bearings, simultaneous north and east on the same native tile, steady playback on turning, retirement of just the east voice when walking away, continuous extension and the footstep toggle. The Windows fixture verified the four pitch identities, stereo and HRTF placement and seams, the noise bursts and gap, concurrent playback advancing all four native wall voices, independent revocation and continued footstep output, expiry and callback removal with the silent driver. The matching NW fixture verified the packaged previews and persisted settings. Actual audibility, device timing and total application overhead remain gameplay checks. ## Dependencies and payload check

The builder defaults to the checkout's `.deps/audio-sdk` and accepts `-AudioSdkRoot` for an equivalent local source. It verifies the key input hashes before staging the two runtime libraries and their notices in `audio-deps`. OpenAL Soft includes its MIT KEMAR measurements in the DLL. The matching corresponding-source URL and measurement attribution travel in `audio-deps/SOURCE.txt`. The Windows release must preserve those files and the SDL license. The current platform target is Windows x64. [SDL 3.4.4](https://github.com/libsdl-org/SDL/releases/tag/release-3.4.4) · [OpenAL Soft 1.25.2 HRTF](https://github.com/kcat/openal-soft/blob/1.25.2/docs/hrtf.txt) · [MIT KEMAR measurements and terms](https://sound.media.mit.edu/resources/KEMAR.html)

Static PE inspection confirmed x64 for the addon and both audio SDKs. The addon exports `napi_register_module_v1` and imports `KERNEL32.dll`. The SDKs' static imports are Windows system libraries.
