# On-map quest tracker

When the tracker shows a quest, the mod speaks the displayed title, when available, and the next unfinished objective with its displayed count. In **Speech**, enable **Autodetail** to hear the visible objective list after that announcement. **Menu indexing** adds the line position and count when reviewing. Both settings default off.

On the map, press **I** to read the visible tracker, including the objectives and their displayed counts. **Shift+Up** and **Shift+Down** move through the lines. **Shift+Left** and **Shift+Right** jump to the first and last line. On an Xbox layout, press and release **View**, then **Y** to read details or use the D-pad in mod mode to review. The controller returns to ordinary input after the action. The controls can be remapped. An existing custom binding can move or leave a new quest binding unassigned.

The shortcut is listed under **F8 → Keyboard bindings → Read details**. The tracker must be visible for on-demand review.

The small tracker shows objectives. An expanded tracker shows the title too. Once the title has been shown for a quest, it stays in review when that quest collapses in the same tracker window. A new quest starts review at its first line. During native dialogue, use the dialogue's own repeat command. Quest controls become available when the field resumes.

## Installed producers and ownership

The installed game has its library journal menu command and shortcut disabled. The observed game route is the tracker created by `Scene_Map.createQuestNavigation`, which adds a `Window_QuestMapNavigation` to the native window layer. The game sets its navigation quest, chooses whether to expand it, and moves the tracker offscreen when hidden. [Quest entry audit](menus.md#quest-entry-and-tracker-trace)

On Steam build 26578620 the fingerprinted quest plugin paints the tracker in `drawNavContents` at original byte 157497. The transform observes settled paints at 157600–158215. A short-lived wrapper on that window's own bitmap records the title and native objective rows at their draw calls. The native root-quest painter draws completed rows and the first incomplete row. Other quest types draw all rows selected by their native painter. The game owns clear-note and counter visibility. The mod uses the actual draws and their geometry and applies no rules based on unrevealed objectives or quest names. [Registration transform](../tools/registration.py) · [Paint observer](../src/menus/quest-paint.js)

The tracker stages at most 24 text draws of at most 96 characters from a paint. It restores the bitmap method at paint exit, including on a native exception. After the settled map update at byte 369098, one removable postrender listener checks that the same window, contents, revision and quest are visible. The observer checks the client area and viewport. The current review holds up to eight lines. Repeated equal-text rows stay in order. Native semantic refresh at 375124 revokes the previous page before repaint. A fold revokes speech during motion and preserves the same quest's review position. It can announce a title on its first settled reveal.

The field and tracker retain native references on the renderer. The speech service receives only bounded output records. Hiding, focus loss, resize, settings, events and scene departure revoke current output. The tracker can capture a native paint during a settled visible dialogue wait, then publish only when the field is ready. Transfer, fade, save, resource and event-entry gates prevent tracker paint sampling in protected phases. ## Restoring the visible tracker

Native destruction saves the target fold state in `Game_Temp`. A replacement tracker can paint while loading and then sit in the small state without another redraw. A tracker refreshed while slid offscreen can also show the already-painted contents. After field handback, the mod waits for postrender and checks the same staged window, completed native paint, contents bitmap, ready font and active navigation quest before recovering these pages.

The recovery uses the installed title and objective painters on a detached receiver. Text placement uses the installed MonkeyWrench and Eli bitmap font methods. The receiver measures from the ready glyph atlas and captures text draw requests. Its bitmap writes, background drawing and icon loading are inert. It has no native sprite, canvas or input listener. The captured page goes through the same visibility and review checks as a regular paint. The plugin verifies the fingerprint of the installed font implementation when building registration candidates. [Recovery adapter](../src/menus/quest-restore.js) · [Registration checks](../tools/registration.py)

The pinned quest registry contains 35 titles and 40 objective strings, with at most three objectives per quest. The longest title is 26 characters and the longest objective is 33. The shipped strings are plain ASCII and the configured clear notes are empty. Recovery bounds older save rows, numbers and line counts and rejects escape substitutions. A rejected recovery waits for a new native paint before retrying. Root-quest branches and title visibility remain the responsibility of the same native methods used for the on-screen tracker.

## Offline evidence and next checks

It checks root and nonroot reveal, clear notes, fold cursor, clipping, stale speech retirement and bounded listener ownership. It exercises an expanded tracker painted during initial save entry, a collapsed tracker carried into a new map, and a revision painted offscreen. The cases verify that restoring these pages leaves the native paint count, draw receipt and bitmap method unchanged. ## Following quest markers

Use the **Quests** category when the game has an eligible marker on the current map. Press **Shift+J** or **Shift+L** to change category, **J** or **L** to choose a destination, **K** to read it, and **P** for directions. On a controller, use the right stick to browse and the left trigger for directions. The existing alternate keyboard layout and remaps apply. [All navigation controls](navigation.md#destination-controls-in-the-development-build)

An actionable marker leads to the current event's normal interaction approach. A marked prop or scenery waypoint leads to its walkable tile or the edge of its solid footprint. Use the quest tracker for the objective text. A quest chest is still read by treasure type. A marked scene trigger is listed only in **Quests**. An ordinary object, chest, character or exit can still be found by its own category. **All** lists the target once.

Only two quest definitions have place text, and the installed tracker's draw routine leaves its library place renderer unused. The 239-map audit found 40 authored marker commands, eight quest event signals, 18 explicit objective completions and 87 automatic-marker suppressions. The most common objective type is event-driven, with 36 of 40 rows. Place text alone cannot identify the active destination.

`Game_Event.setupFlagIcons` at original byte 332464 indexes marker commands from current pages and can also index quest signals and completions from later pages. `Spriteset_Map.createQuestFlagIcons` at 342690 builds the attached marker sprites. `Sprite_AyatamQuestIcon.setMapIcon` at 345891 owns active quest, unfinished objective and report eligibility. The native edge arrow still points to the linked event. Generated groups can cover several events. The mod resolves each member against its effective page, so an ordinary switch page remains an ordinary interaction until its quest command becomes active. The route uses the live event tile or footprint. [Marker reader](../src/world/quest-destinations.js)

The renderer samples marker ownership only when a navigation command reaches a ready postrender. The snapshot keeps a maximum of 128 sprites, 512 group members and 8,192 event commands. The installed static audit found a maximum of 95 marker-related commands on one map across all its pages. The worker receives copied geometry, a numeric event ID and the waypoint choice. Directions, beacon and autowalk validate the same renderer-owned event page, marker, quest and map. The pre-refresh notification at original byte 343956 retires active quest navigation before native sprite removal and its asset waiter. Normal event entry, field invalidation and scene departure also revoke the route. [Field ownership](../src/world/field.js) · [Route goals](../src/world/routes.cjs)

The installed-method fixture follows a marked chest and its spent page, then checks that the gate switches become quest destinations only on their eligible pages. It verifies the transition from those switches to a solid marked prop, with a walkable approach and no activation instruction. The packaged worker routes matched the extracted native movement and action checks. On the bounded runner the two cases took 41.9–43.6 ms cold and 0.7–1.4 ms warm through the worker. In one comparison, 120 Map 015 browse snapshots took 8.08 ms with the marker reader bypassed and 8.04 ms connected. The matching NW.js fixture verifies selection, spoken directions and retirement of both beacon and autowalk on marker replacement. Game timing and audible behavior remain to be confirmed. [Installed-method fixture](../tests/quest-destinations.cjs) · [Matching NW.js fixture](../tests/nw-smoke.py)

At final gameplay handoff, cycle to **Quests** after accepting a quest with a marker. Request directions and use the ordinary game control at the destination. After progressing the objective, reread the category and request directions again. Expect the current marker or an unavailable selection when the old marker has gone. The current-map browser checks reachable approaches. Cross-map routes remain in the world checkpoint. [Navigation and remaining scope](navigation.md#reachable-destinations-on-the-current-map)

Standalone quest markers are listed under **Quests** while the game displays them. An interactable object or character can also appear under its own category.
